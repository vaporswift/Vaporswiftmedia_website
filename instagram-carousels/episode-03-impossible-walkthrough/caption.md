Drone shot that flies at the house, through the front door, and keeps going into the living room. One unbroken take. No FPV pilot, no permit, no second attempt.

Here's the part people get wrong: I never described the camera move.

Text is a terrible way to specify motion. "Slow push-in" has a hundred valid readings and the model picks one at random, so you re-roll and re-roll and never quite get the shot you saw in your head.

Instead, give it two pictures.

start_image — the twilight exterior.
end_image — the lit interior.

Now the model isn't inventing a shot. It's solving for the path between two frames you already chose. Composition is locked at both ends, and the prompt shrinks to describing motion only.

Full prompt, all of it:

One continuous flying shot, no cuts. The camera glides forward toward the house, rises slightly, and passes through the open front door without stopping — carrying straight on into the warm lit interior and settling on the living space.

Smooth FPV drone motion, constant forward momentum, never pausing at the threshold. Cool twilight light gives way to warm interior light as we cross inside. Quiet evening ambience.

No people. No text.

That's the whole thing. Five lines.

The six words doing the heavy lifting: "never pausing at the threshold." Leave them out and the model reads the doorway as a cut point — you get two shots stitched together instead of one continuous move, and the entire effect dies. The effect WAS the continuity.

One more thing worth noticing. The start frame is the twilight exterior from Episode 01. Nothing new was shot for this. Your stills library is the raw material for video, which means every image you make is now two assets.

Save this for the next listing you've already photographed.
