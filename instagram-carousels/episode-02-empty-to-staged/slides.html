PART 2 OF 2 — THE TELL
LIGHT: the furniture must obey the light
already in the room. Overcast daylight
enters from the windows on the left wall,
so every shadow falls to the right, soft
and long. Dark contact shadows where legs,
rug edge and console meet the floor.
Nothing is lit from a direction the room
does not have.

MEDIUM: interiors photography, Architectural
Digest, 24mm tilt-shift, f/8, verticals
corrected. Subtle grain.

NOT: no people, no clutter, no floating
furniture, no shadow pointing the wrong
way, no changes to the room itself.

Why it works

Staging is a
lighting problem.

ONLY ADD
Two words that save the shot. Without them the model repaints your walls and swaps the flooring — and now you're advertising a house that doesn't exist.
NAME IT ALL
"Furniture" gives you a generic showroom. A named list of eight specific pieces gives you a room someone lives in.
LIGHT
This is the whole episode. Fake staging is spotted because the sofa is lit from a window that isn't there. Say which wall the light comes from and which way shadows fall.
CONTACT
Furniture with no contact shadow floats. Ask for ambient occlusion where legs meet floor and the whole frame sits down.
SCALE
"Rug under the sofa's front legs" is a real staging rule. A rug floating in open floor reads as wrong scale instantly.
MEDIUM
Verticals corrected again — the same tell as Episode 01, and the same fix.
NOT
Name the failure out loud: no shadow pointing the wrong way.
