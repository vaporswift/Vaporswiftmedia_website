# Episode 02 — Caption

---

Virtually stage an empty listing with one prompt. Same room, same photo, furniture only.

Physical staging runs into the thousands and takes a week to book. This took one pass on a photo the agent already had.

The mistake almost everyone makes is treating this as a furniture problem. It isn't. It's a lighting problem.

Fake staging gets spotted for one reason: the sofa is lit from a window the room doesn't have. Your eye catches it before your brain does — the shadows point the wrong way, and the whole photo reads as a lie even if you can't say why.

So you tell the model where the light already is:

"Overcast daylight enters from the windows on the left wall, so every shadow falls to the right, soft and long."

Then you nail it to the floor:

"Dark contact shadows where legs, rug edge and console meet the floor."

Furniture with no contact shadow floats. That one line is the difference between a room and a collage.

Two more that carry weight:

→ ONLY ADD FURNISHINGS. Leave it out and the model repaints your walls and swaps your flooring — and now you're advertising a house that doesn't exist. That's not a bad render, that's a problem.

→ "Rug under the sofa's front legs." A real staging rule. A rug floating in open floor reads as wrong scale instantly, and it's the second thing people notice after the shadows.

Full prompt, copy it:

PRESERVE: this is the exact same room. Keep the walls, floor, windows, blinds, ceiling, doorway, proportions and camera position exactly as the source image. Do not change the architecture. Only add furnishings.

STAGE IT AS: a warm minimalist living room. Low oatmeal-linen three-seat sofa, round travertine coffee table, pale wool rug with its front edge under the sofa's front legs, slim black arc floor lamp, walnut console beneath the window with two ceramic vessels, olive tree in a clay pot, two abstract prints in thin oak frames.

LIGHT: the furniture must obey the light already in the room. Overcast daylight enters from the windows on the left wall, so every shadow falls to the right, soft and long. Dark contact shadows where legs, rug edge and console meet the floor. Nothing is lit from a direction the room does not have.

MEDIUM: interiors photography, Architectural Digest, 24mm tilt-shift, f/8, verticals corrected. Subtle grain.

NOT: no people, no clutter, no floating furniture, no shadow pointing the wrong way, no changes to the room itself.

Swap only the STAGE IT AS line and you stage the same room for three different buyers — minimalist for the young professional, mid-century for the design buyer, soft and durable for the family. Stage for who's buying, not for your own taste.

One thing that isn't optional: virtually staged photos have to be disclosed under MLS and NAR rules. Label them, and never alter the room itself — furniture only. Do it right and this is a tool. Do it wrong and it's a complaint.

Save this before your next vacant listing.

Send it to the agent sitting on an empty one that won't move.
