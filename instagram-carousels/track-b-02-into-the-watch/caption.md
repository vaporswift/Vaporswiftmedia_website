The camera goes through the sapphire crystal and keeps travelling until it's sitting among the gears — one unbroken shot, and I never described the move.

Here's how.

You give it two pictures instead of a paragraph.

start_image — the hero shot on slate.
end_image — extreme macro inside the movement.

The model isn't inventing a shot any more. It's solving for the path between two frames you chose. Composition locked at both ends, so the prompt only has to describe motion.

That's the whole prompt, and it's six lines:

One continuous shot, no cuts. The camera pushes slowly forward toward the watch, then travels through the sapphire crystal and keeps going down into the movement, ending among the gears.

Constant forward momentum, scale shrinking the whole way. Specular highlights sweep across the crystal as we pass through it, then the light becomes tight and raking on the metal inside. Soft mechanical ticking.

No people, no hands. No text.

Six words are carrying the shot: "scale shrinking the whole way."

Leave them out and the model cuts to the macro instead of travelling into it — and you lose the only thing that made it impossible. The unbroken change in scale IS the trick.

Why this beats writing a better camera description: "push in slowly" has a hundred valid readings and the model picks one at random. So you re-roll, and re-roll, and never land the shot you saw. Two frames end the guessing.

Worth saying plainly: no watch was photographed. Both frames were generated, then pinned as first and last. If you have a real product, shoot it and use your own frames — the technique doesn't care where the stills came from.

And pick moves that are expensive or impossible, not merely pretty. Pretty AI video is a commodity now. Impossible isn't.
