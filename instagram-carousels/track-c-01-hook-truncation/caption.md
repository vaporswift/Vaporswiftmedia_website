# Track C 01 — Caption

Carousel: vapor bed + floating text is position 1, then 4 cards.

This caption is itself the demo — the first line is 104 characters. Count it.

---

Instagram cuts your caption off around 125 characters, and it doesn't stop at a word — it cuts mid-word.

Here's how to fix it in one prompt.

Go look at your last post right now. Whatever sits above the "… more" is the entire pitch you actually made. Everything under it only exists for people who already decided to care.

Most captions bury the good line in paragraph three.

So don't write a better caption. Make the one you already wrote start in the right place:

Rewrite my Instagram caption so the hook survives truncation.

1. First sentence lands the whole hook in under 125 characters.
2. It creates a gap — a claim, a number, a contradiction. Not a summary.
3. Then "Here's how." and straight into the method.
4. Keep every fact. Change nothing true.
5. No emoji openers, no "In today's world", no throat-clearing.

Give me three options, each with the character count of its first sentence.

CAPTION: [paste yours]

That's the whole thing.

Rule 4 is the one that matters. Without it you get a rewrite that sounds punchier and quietly stops being true — a number rounds up, a claim gets bolder, and now you're defending something you didn't mean. Pinning it to the facts turns the job into what it should have been: finding the sentence that was already in there and moving it to the front.

Rule 5 exists because the throat-clearing is what eats the 125.

Here's a real one from this account:

Before — 147 characters, cut mid-word:
"We've been experimenting with AI for real estate marketing lately and wanted to share something we learned about virtual staging that surprised us."

After — 123 characters, lands whole:
"Staging this room physically costs thousands and takes a week to book — this took one pass on a photo that already existed."

Nothing was invented. The second line was already sitting in paragraph three of the first draft.

Run it on something you've already published. That's the fastest way to see it work — you know the facts are right, so the only thing you're judging is whether the first line earns the tap.

Save this. You will not retype it from memory at 11pm.

Send it to someone whose captions deserve to be read.
