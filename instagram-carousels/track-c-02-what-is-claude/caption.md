Everyone tells you to use Claude. Nobody shows you the actual window. Here are its four parts.

Here's the map.

Most people open it, type a question, get a good answer, and close the tab. That's the chat — one of four parts, and the least interesting one. The other three are why people who use it every day talk about it differently.

Projects. A folder that remembers. Load it with the files it should always have read — your brand guide, your rate card, the last three things you wrote in your own voice — plus standing instructions on how to behave. Every chat you start inside that project already knows all of it. No re-briefing. No re-uploading. No "as I mentioned earlier."

Artifacts. When the output is a real thing — a doc, a page, a table — it opens in a panel beside the chat instead of scrolling past in the conversation. You keep asking for changes in plain language and it updates in place, keeping every version. Round four went too far? Go back to two.

The input. Drop in PDFs, images, spreadsheets. That's where the file goes, and where you pick which model answers.

Set up one Project and the tool stops being a search box.

Here's a starting point for the instructions field. Fill in the brackets:

I am [what you do, in one line]. I work with [who].

VOICE
- Write like [describe two people or brands you sound like].
- Short sentences. Specific over clever.
- Never use: [your list of banned words and phrases].

DEFAULTS
- Give me the work first, explanation after.
- If a fact is missing, ask me — don't invent one.
- If something won't work, say so before you build it.

FILES
- Everything in this project's knowledge is true and current. Prefer it over what you assume.

That's it. Ten minutes, once.

Load the knowledge files second. The three you re-paste most often are the three that belong there — you already know which they are, because you're tired of finding them.

One honest note: there's a free tier, and paid tiers exist. Prices and the model lineup both move, so check the site rather than trusting a carousel — including this one.

The diagrams here are diagrams, not screenshots. The layout is the part worth learning; the pixels change.

Save this for the next time you open it and stare at a blank box.

Send it to someone who tried it once and closed it again.

## Notes

- First line is **94 characters** — re-count if you edit it. `check-caption.js`
  measures it.
- No prices, no model names on any slide. Both move faster than a carousel
  ages, and a wrong number on a teaching post costs more than the slide is
  worth. The caption points at the source instead.
- Slides 2–4 are drawn diagrams, labelled as such on-slide. A real screenshot
  is unreadable at carousel scale — the interesting parts are 11px type — and
  it's stale the week the UI moves. If you'd rather use real screenshots, drop
  them in `assets/` and swap the `.win` block for an `<img>`; the pin and
  legend system works over either.
- Slide 2 is the second-serve cover. It carries "SLIDE 3" and "SLIDE 4"
  pointers inside the legend, which is the swipe driver for this post.
